2 · Think first, then reveal

A person or thing

Which word describes the person: a careful student or a carefully student?

Reveal explanation

A careful student. Careful describes student (a noun), so use an adjective. Ask: what kind of student?

How an action happens

The student checks careful / carefully. Which word tells us how?

Reveal explanation

The student checks carefully. Carefully describes how the student checks, so use an adverb. Many manner adverbs add -ly: quiet → quietly, careful → carefully.

Read before the mini-test · Feel, look, sound, smell and taste

She looks calm / calmly. She looks careful / carefully at the plan. What changes?

Reveal explanation

She looks calm: calm describes her state. She looks carefully at the plan: carefully describes the action of looking. Use adjectives after be and after feel/look/sound/smell/taste when they describe the subject: the soup tastes good; I feel happy. Do not just look at the word immediately before the gap.

Good and well; spelling

A good / well player plays good / well. How do happy and easy change?

Reveal explanation

A good player plays well. Good describes the player; well describes the playing. Happy → happily; easy → easily (change -y to -ily). Note: well can also be an adjective meaning healthy: I feel well.

Read before the mini-test · Do not add -ly automatically

Complete: a fast runner runs ___. We work ___ (hard). A friendly teacher. Are all -ly words adverbs?

Reveal explanation

A fast runner runs fast. We work hard: with a lot of effort. Fast and hard can be adjectives or adverbs, with the same form. Hardly is a different word: it means almost not (German: kaum). We hardly work = we almost don’t work. Friendly is an adjective despite its -ly ending: a friendly teacher; she speaks in a friendly way. Adverbs can also describe adjectives or other adverbs: really tired; very carefully.
