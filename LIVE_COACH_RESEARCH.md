# Existing work informing the live teaching prototype

Reviewed 17 October 2026. This is a source-backed design comparison, not a hands-on product evaluation. No third-party code has been copied into this implementation.

This does not establish automatic two-speaker recognition for two people sharing a room microphone. |

## Practical decisions carried into the implementation

- The AI returns a faithful excerpt, one concrete strength, one priority, a minimal correction only when justified, a question Rory can ask, and a next step. It must express uncertainty and recognise successful independent retries.
- The running commentary distinguishes the learner, Rory and AI. Microphone identity in the first version is teacher-selected or unassigned, not falsely advertised as automatic diarisation. Each caption can be relabelled; corrections are supplied back to the ongoing AI conversation.
- PDF slide images and extracted text supply current-page context. Text extraction alone cannot represent a diagram or image-only exercise.
- The teacher's commentary and questions are excluded from the slide-only audience channel. For online lessons, share the audience window through an existing video call. Same-browser audience synchronisation is distinct from remote iPad pairing or a built-in video-call service.
- Begin with response instructions and concrete examples. This version does not train or fine-tune a new model. Any future fine-tuning needs evidence that prompting and retrieval fail on recurring real examples.
- A meaningful lesson test should examine misheard errors, incorrect speaker attribution, age-appropriate explanations, response timing, source alignment, and whether Rory actually uses the suggestions. Compile/test success alone does not establish instructional benefit.

Automatic speaker recognition remains an unresolved test requirement. Separate microphone streams can preserve known identities more reliably than guessing names from one mixed audio stream; they introduce an additional connection workflow. Same-room microphone placement, turn overlap and online-call audio routing need device testing before choosing that architecture.
